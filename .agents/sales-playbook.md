# VR Academy Sales Playbook

## Positioning

One-line pitch:

VR Academy helps companies rehearse dangerous safety procedures in VR before workers face real risk.

Short pitch:

VR Academy combines VR scenarios, worker records, course assignment, results, certificates, and certificate verification so companies can standardize safety training across teams, sites, and shifts.

What not to say:

- do not sell it as a game
- do not promise legal certification unless the certification framework is agreed in writing
- do not use old deck pricing externally
- do not say all six scenarios are ready until confirmed by the owner
- do not promise that VR Academy replaces mandatory workplace safety training

## Pilot Offer

Default pilot:

- 1 scenario
- 1 worker group
- 25 to 40 participants when the buyer has enough volume, or up to 10 participants for a small proof of concept
- setup of company account and worker list
- demo or guided training session
- completion report and certificate workflow
- recommendation for rollout

Indicative pilot framing:

- 30 days
- one demo-ready scenario
- agreed success criteria
- final pilot report
- indicative range: 1,500 to 3,000 EUR when scope includes guided pilot work; otherwise use the public 29.99 EUR per participant per assigned course model

Pilot buying question:

Which safety procedure would you most want people to practice before they face it in the real environment?

## Discovery Questions

1. Which safety training is repeated most often?
2. Which incident type would be most expensive or dangerous if handled badly?
3. How do you currently prove who completed training?
4. Do different sites or instructors deliver training differently?
5. How many workers need this training per year?
6. Which roles, shifts, and locations are in scope?
7. What records or certificates must management see?
8. Is the first goal internal validation, compliance support, or rollout across teams?
9. Who provides VR devices, space, and onsite operational support?
10. Who must approve IT/security, procurement, and legal terms?

## Objection Handling

"We already do classroom training."

Response: VR Academy is not replacing every classroom session. It adds controlled practice for decisions, sequence, and reaction under pressure, then records the result.

"VR sounds expensive."

Response: The pilot starts with one scenario and one worker group, so the company can compare the cost against incident risk, instructor time, repeated training, and audit records before scaling.

"We need compliance."

Response: VR Academy can record completion, results, certificates, and verification. Formal compliance mapping should be scoped with the buyer's required standard before it is promised externally.

"Workers are not technical."

Response: The system is designed around scenario-based practice, not complex software use. The pilot should include a representative worker group to validate usability.

"We need custom scenarios."

Response: Custom scenarios are possible after the first training area is defined. The first step is to document process, risk points, equipment, decisions, and success criteria.
